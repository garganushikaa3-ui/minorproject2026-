Project Overview

PathWise: Decision Support and Career Path Explorer

An explainable, data-driven career decision-support system designed for undergraduate computer applications (BCA) students.

System Overview
Platform: PathWise Decision Support System
Discipline: Computer Applications & Software Engineering
Audience: BCA & Undergraduate Computing Students
Version: v1.0 (Production Ready)

1. Problem Statement & Motivation

Undergraduate technical students face a crowded, rapidly shifting landscape of technical roles—ranging from traditional Software Engineering and Web Development to modern Cloud, DevOps, Data Science, and Cybersecurity. Students frequently experience decision paralysis due to:

  • Lack of consolidated, unbiased career information in one place.
  • Vague job descriptions that do not clarify day-to-day work tasks or required entry skills.
  • Misleading salary claims and unverified job promises from commercial bootcamps.
  • Overconfidence or underconfidence in self-rated skills without practical task evidence.
  • Lack of a structured, step-by-step roadmap from current BCA semester to career readiness.

2. System Objectives

PathWise bridges these gaps through a five-pillar software architecture:

Unified Exploration

Consolidates 38 verified technical occupations with official O*NET essential skills, tools, and labor market data.

Explainable Scoring

Calculates transparent match scores using configurable weights across Interests (30%), Skills (30%), Tools (15%), Education (15%), and Preferences (10%).

Practical Trial Labs

Provides small, safe problem-solving tasks in Data Analytics, Software Logic, Cybersecurity, and UX to test hands-on intuition.

What-If Simulation

Allows hypothetical experimentation with skills, priority weights, and tools without permanently modifying saved student records.

4. Viva Demonstration Guide

Unlike opaque black-box machine learning models, PathWise uses an explicit, multi-criteria weighted scoring formula. For every career, the student sees exact contribution percentages (Interests, Skills, Tools, Education, Preferences), the specific skills that matched, the exact tools missing, and an explicit 'Why Not This Career?' diagnostic module.

Students often rate their skills based on theoretical coursework (e.g. self-rating Python 4/5). The Career Trial Lab presents practical domain tasks (e.g. finding a discount bug, interpreting cafeteria sales anomalies). The Evidence Check compares self-ratings with lab outcomes respectfully, highlighting areas for practice without discouragement or silent overwrites.

PathWise strictly avoids mixing global and domestic benchmarks. U.S. BLS 2025 figures are clearly labeled in USD ($) with year and source link. Separate, clearly identified India Tech Industry entry-to-mid career ranges (in INR Lakhs Per Annum) are provided to ensure students have realistic local expectations.
